Data cleaning

Clean up the data. Ensure categorical variables are coded as factors as this makes it much easier to use in statistical models and graphs. Age, year, and time-to-event variables are coded as numeric. “Unknown” or missing data is coded as NA. Invalid values are also coded as NA.

# the original `Race` variable (added in 1989) was archived in 2023 and is therefore not available in the publicly available dataset. During data cleaning I created a categorical variable for race using `RaceWhtF`, `RaceBlkF`, `RacePIF`, `RaceIndF` and `RaceAsnF` and those who identified with more than 1 race.
## categorise race
longitudinal_complete <- longitudinal_complete %>%
  mutate(
    RaceIndF_C = ifelse(RaceIndF > 1, NA, RaceIndF),
    RaceBlkF_C = ifelse(RaceBlkF > 1, NA, RaceBlkF),
    RacePIF_C = ifelse(RacePIF > 1, NA, RacePIF),
    RaceAsnF_C = ifelse(RaceAsnF > 1, NA, RaceAsnF),
    RaceWhtF_C = ifelse(RaceWhtF > 1, NA, RaceWhtF)
  ) %>%
  mutate(
    RaceCount =  RaceIndF_C + RaceBlkF_C + RacePIF_C + RaceAsnF_C + RaceWhtF_C,
    RaceCat = case_when(
      RaceCount == 1 & RaceWhtF_C == 1 ~ 1, # white
      RaceCount == 1 & RaceBlkF_C == 1 ~ 2, # black
      RaceCount == 1 & RacePIF_C == 1 ~ 3, # PIF
      RaceCount == 1 & RaceAsnF_C == 1 ~ 4, # Asian 
      RaceCount == 1 & RaceIndF_C == 1 ~ 5, # AIAN
      RaceCount > 1 ~ 6,  #mixed race category
      is.na(RaceBlkF_C) & is.na(RacePIF_C) & is.na(RaceAsnF_C) & is.na(RaceIndF_C) & !is.na(RaceWhtF_C) ~ 1, # white
      !is.na(RaceBlkF_C) & is.na(RaceWhtF_C) & is.na(RacePIF_C) & is.na(RaceAsnF_C) & is.na(RaceIndF_C) ~ 2, # black
    !is.na(RacePIF_C) & is.na(RaceWhtF_C) & is.na(RaceBlkF_C) & is.na(RaceAsnF_C) & is.na(RaceIndF_C) ~ 3, # NHPI
    !is.na(RaceAsnF_C) & is.na(RaceWhtF_C) & is.na(RaceBlkF_C) & is.na(RacePIF_C) & is.na(RaceIndF_C) ~ 4, # Asian 
    !is.na(RaceIndF_C) & is.na(RaceWhtF_C) & is.na(RaceBlkF_C) & is.na(RacePIF_C) & is.na(RaceAsnF_C) ~ 5, # AIAN
    )
  ) %>%
  mutate(Race1 = case_when(
    RaceCat == 1 ~ 1, # white
    RaceCat == 2 ~ 2, # black
    RaceCat == 4 ~ 3, # asian
    RaceCat == 3 ~ 4, # NHPI
    RaceCat == 5 ~ 5, # AIAN
    RaceCat == 6 ~ 6 # mixed race
  )) %>%
  mutate(RaceCat = as.factor(RaceCat)) %>%
  mutate(Race1 = as.factor(Race1))

      EthnicityF_C == 1 & is.na(Race1) ~ "Non-Hispanic (Race Not Reported)",

      is.na(EthnicityF_C) & Race1 == 2 ~ "Black (Ethnicity Not Reported)",

      is.na(EthnicityF_C) & Race1 == 5 ~ "AIAN (Ethnicity Not Reported)",
      is.na(EthnicityF_C) & Race1 == 6 ~ "Mixed Race (Ethnicity Not Reported)",

      # neither race or ethnicity reported
      TRUE ~ NA_character_
    )
  ) %>%
  
  # NIH reporting categories
  mutate(
    race_ethnicity_C = case_when(
      # Hispanic ALWAYS overrides race
      str_starts(race_ethnicity, "Hispanic") ~ "Hispanic or Latino",
      
      # to reduce missingness → Non-Hispanic other is a category
      str_detect(race_ethnicity, "Non-Hispanic \\(Race Not Reported\\)") ~ "Non-Hispanic Other",

      # race-based NIH categories
      str_detect(race_ethnicity, "Asian") | str_detect(race_ethnicity, "NHPI") ~ "Asian / NHPI",
      str_detect(race_ethnicity, "Black") ~ "Black or AA",
      str_detect(race_ethnicity, "White") ~ "White",
      str_detect(race_ethnicity, "AIAN") ~ "AIAN",

      # If mixed and non-Hispanic → NIH would classify as "More than one race"
      str_detect(race_ethnicity, "Mixed") ~ "More than one race",

      TRUE ~ "Unknown"
    )
  ) %>%

  mutate(race_ethnicity_C = case_when(
    race_ethnicity_C == "White" ~ 1,
    race_ethnicity_C == "Black or AA" ~ 2,
    race_ethnicity_C == "Asian / NHPI" ~ 3,
    race_ethnicity_C == "Hispanic or Latino" ~ 4,
    race_ethnicity_C == "AIAN" ~ 5,
    race_ethnicity_C == "Non-Hispanic Other" ~ 6,
    race_ethnicity_C == "More than one race" ~ 7,
    race_ethnicity_C == "Unknown" ~ NA

Assessing for multicollinearity

An important step in the logistic regression analysis is to assess the assumption of no strong multicollinearity. To do so, we used the car::viff() function to calculate the variance inflation factor (VIF), which was pooled across all imputed datasets.

For the car::viff() output, the generalized VIF is found in the GVIF column, and the GVIF^(1/(2*Df)) column is the adjusted generalized standard error inflation factor (aGSIF). It is recommended that the aGSIF value to evaluate collinearity, since it allows predictors with different Df (different number of terms in the model) to be comparable (Nahhas, 2024).

For aGSIFs, our rule of thumb for what is large is that values above 1.6 may be of concern and values above 2.2 or 3.2 are indicative of a more serious problem (Nahhas, 2024).
